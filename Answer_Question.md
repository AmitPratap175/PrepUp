# MBA Application Essay

**Question:** Over the past year, have you actively pursued opportunities to enhance your knowledge in any field? If so, please elaborate on your efforts. If not, what steps could you have taken to further your learning?

**Answer:**

Over the past year, I actively pursued a dual-track enhancement of my technical product skills and my competitive aptitude for MBA entrance exams. I achieved this not by treating them as separate pursuits, but by building **PrepUp**, a learning platform where I was both the Chief Architect and the Primary User. My key insight was that standard study methods were passive; I needed *active, engineered* interventions to bridge my personal learning gaps. I successfully implemented this through five interconnected mechanisms:

The genesis of PrepUp emerged from a critical gap I identified during my tenure at Rebhu Computing, a deep-tech startup where I was an early employee. While I gained invaluable experience in product building—reconciling technical development across engineering, product, and client teams—I realized my understanding of **end-to-end deployment and scalability** remained superficial. I could architect features, but I lacked the holistic view of how products survive in production under real user load. Simultaneously, as I prepared for competitive MBA exams, I observed that contemporary free preparation websites were fundamentally broken: they offered static question banks without intelligent revision, lacked contextual learning tools, and provided zero behavioral scaffolding for discipline. This convergence of personal need and market gap presented the perfect opportunity. By building PrepUp from scratch—handling everything from database architecture to DevOps pipelines to AI integration—I would force myself to master the deployment and scalability challenges I had only observed at Rebhu. But the true strategic insight was **being my own primary user**: this created an unfiltered feedback loop that exposed every friction point instantly. If the spaced repetition algorithm failed me, I couldn't ignore it—I felt the consequence directly in my exam performance. This dual accountability—as both builder and user—transformed PrepUp from a learning project into a strategic product that, I believe, addresses the critical deficiencies plaguing free MBA preparation platforms today.


First, to master Verbal Ability, I rejected the inefficiency of static word lists and built a **Contextual Thesaurus & Reviewer**. I realized that knowing a definition is useless without understanding usage. I engineered a tool that allowed me to click any word during practice for an instant, in-screen definition, but with a critical twist: it saved the *entire sentence* as context. This created a personalized "Contextual Thesaurus" that I reviewed daily. By revisiting words *in the situations I encountered them*, rather than in isolation, I dramatically improved my retention of nuance—a distinct USP that standard dictionaries lack.

Second, I tackled the challenge of Critical Writing for XAT's Decision-Making section by building an **AI Essay Evaluator**. Rather than relying on expensive human coaching, I codified the exact grading rubric—evaluating essays on Coherence, Argumentation, and Structure. By programming the AI to grade my work, I was forced to deeply understand *what makes a high-scoring essay*. This meta-learning—teaching the machine before I could master it myself—accelerated my writing skills 5x faster than traditional methods, with instant feedback in 10-20 seconds versus days of waiting for tutor reviews.

Third, I engineered a solution to the "Forgetting Curve" using **Algorithmic Spaced Repetition**. I found myself repeating mistakes in Quantitative Aptitude, so I built a robust **Bookmarking System** that wasn't just a passive "save" button. I programmed the system to generate **"Custom Tests from Bookmarks"**, forcing me to re-solve only the specific problems I had struggled with. More critically, I implemented a fibonacci-interval scheduler (2, 4, 6 days) that automatically force-fed me overdue revision questions before allowing me to attempt new topics. This turned my "Error Log" into an interactive, dynamic exam that ensured systematic closure of every knowledge gap.

Fourth, I addressed Information Retention for General Knowledge by building a **PDF-to-Quiz RAG Agent** using NotebookLM. I would upload daily newspaper PDFs (like The Indian Express), and the system would automatically generate "UPSC-style" questions—Statement-Based Traps, Chronology Sequences, and Match-the-Following formats. By engineering the AI prompt to replicate exam patterns, I trained myself to spot these specific questioning techniques in real exams, dramatically improving my pattern recognition.

Fifth, I systemized my discipline through **Algorithmic Habit Formation**. Recognizing that consistency was my weakness, I coded a **Daily Targets** engine that auto-generated a fresh, bite-sized "Daily Quiz" every morning. I also built an "Early Bird" badge system that specifically rewarded morning login sessions between 6-8 AM. The desire to debug and verify this gamification feature motivated me to wake up at 6 AM consistently. Additionally, I created a **PDF Export Engine** that compiled my bookmarked mistakes into physical "Error Booklets" for offline practice, reducing screen fatigue and simulating the actual OMR exam experience.

Beyond the technical features themselves, building PrepUp end-to-end taught me invaluable lessons in **Product Leadership**. I learned that scalability requires delegation—implementing a "Supervisor Agent" pattern taught me that monolithic approaches create bottlenecks, mirroring how managers must distribute work to specialists. I discovered that **cost optimization is strategic resource allocation**: transitioning from expensive AI models to optimized smaller models for routine tasks (like vocabulary lookup) taught me to balance quality with operational efficiency. Most critically, I learned that **user experience is non-negotiable performance**—when my initial chatbot blocked the UI thread, I realized that perceived responsiveness matters more than raw computational power, leading me to architect asynchronous AI pipelines. Finally, I understood that **market fit demands authentic simulation**: refactoring casual quizzes into strict, timed "formal test formats" taught me that users don't want features—they want to feel the pressure of the real exam. These weren't just coding lessons; they were management principles disguised as technical decisions.

In conclusion, my pursuit of knowledge was defined by **self-validation through direct user experience**—using my own product to survive the rigors of exam prep. By building features like the Contextual Thesaurus, AI Essay Evaluator, Spaced Repetition Scheduler, and PDF-to-Quiz RAG Engine, I didn't just consume knowledge; I engineered the systems required to acquire, retain, and master it. This distinct combination of technical innovation and strategic self-improvement transformed me from a passive learner into an active architect of my own success.

Parallel to PrepUp, I expanded my technical versatility by serving as an **Independent Consultant and Assistant ML Developer at Brahmware**, where I contributed to the development of their flagship **AI Universe** ecosystem. Critically, this wasn't purely backend development—I operated at the intersection of engineering and business, directly engaging with potential customers in requirements-gathering meetings, understanding their operational challenges, and pitching tailored AI solutions. I then translated these business needs into technical architectures, crafting the solutions myself from discovery to deployment. 

My primary focus was on **Neera AI**, a revolutionary WhatsApp-based conversational intelligence suite designed for professionals who "work with people, not machines." I contributed to building both **Neera AI+** (profession-specific assistants for doctors, lawyers, teachers, and consultants where entire business workflows run within a single WhatsApp chat) and the **Neera AI Chatbot** (a deployable white-label engine for enterprises that trains on custom knowledge bases and handles multilingual Indian audiences). This work forced me to master domain-specific workflows across healthcare, education, law, and finance—far beyond my EdTech comfort zone. I was directly involved in designing the tiered pricing strategy (Beginner, Professional, Expert) and the SaaS-vs-lifetime-license business model, learning how pricing architecture itself becomes a product differentiation lever. 

This consulting role validated a crucial insight: while PrepUp taught me to solve *my own* problems deeply through direct user feedback, Brahmware taught me to architect *generalized* solutions for unfamiliar markets through structured requirements discovery and consultative selling. Together, these experiences demonstrate not just technical depth, but strategic versatility across consumer EdTech, enterprise SaaS, ethical AI governance, and go-to-market strategy—precisely the cross-functional fluency required for impactful product leadership.

The transition from a "Pragmatic Builder" to a holistic Product Manager now demands a formal bridge between my technical intuition and corporate strategy. XLRI’s **Post Graduate Diploma in Business Management (PGDBM)** offers the ideal crucible for this transformation. The program's rigorous curriculum, particularly in **Strategic Management and Information Systems**, will help me augment my analytical foundation with the frameworks needed to orchestrate sustainable, scalable growth. Beyond the classroom, I am eager to contribute to XLRI’s uniquely collaborative ecosystem. My background—spanning deep-tech startups, independent consulting, and full-stack product development—equips me to offer distinct perspectives in peer-to-peer learning, particularly in navigating the "Build vs. Scale" paradox. By engaging with XLRI’s distinguished faculty and diverse cohort, I aim to refine my ability to translate technological insights into actionable business outcomes, preparing to lead product teams where innovation must always serve the bottom line.
